# Pricing measurable work

## What to sell now

Make the annual advisory and engineering service the core offer. Commit to a defined service scope, reporting cadence and reserved improvement capacity for a term of at least 12 months. Agree on acceptance criteria for individual work packages inside that relationship. Establish missing baselines through a scoped discovery activity before promising business improvements.

Quote the annual recurring service and PromptForce.AI entitlement first, then any initial or substantial implementation package that exceeds the included capacity. An account with an existing system may need no separate initial build. The fictional example includes one; it is not a compulsory charge for every account. Allocate each activity to exactly one fee line, and do not charge routine improvement work again as a new implementation package.

For suitable work packages, an optional performance fee can represent roughly 10-15% of that package's maximum fee. This is a proposed starting policy, not a market standard. Do not make recurring economics depend on that payment or reset it automatically each year. Monthly service payments are installments under the annual commitment, subject to its service remedies and termination terms. See the [annual service schedule](annual-service-schedule.md).

After several comparable deliveries establish stable cost, quality, volume and attribution, consider a per-successful-transaction model with a setup fee, a minimum commitment, volume bands and a cap. Do not start with an unlimited, no-result/no-payment engineering obligation.

| Commercial model | Where it fits | Main limitation | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed fee for accepted milestones | Work packages exceeding included recurring capacity | Scope and acceptance must be explicit | Used within the continuing relationship |
| Fixed base plus capped performance fee | Repeatable workflow with a signed baseline | Attribution and measurement overhead | Selective |
| Annual advisory and engineering service | Recurring decisions, evaluation and reserved improvement capacity | Scope and capacity must support the promised outputs | Default, with monthly installments |
| Time and materials with a ceiling | Investigation or unstable integration dependencies | Buyer has less final-price certainty | Exception with weekly decisions |
| Price per accepted transaction | Mature, repetitive service with stable economics | Volume, failure, review and dispute exposure | Later, after evidence |
| Share of verified savings | Narrow optimization with auditable spend | Counterfactual savings and client changes are disputable | Only with fixed methodology and fee cap |

## Map the current offers to acceptance

These are proposed scope patterns for the offers on adaptcloud.io, not claims about work already completed.

| Offer | Purchasable outcome | Evidence and acceptance | Pricing basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI Tokenomics Business Analysis | An approved investment decision backed by an agreed cost baseline | Reconciled in-scope spend, documented missing coverage, assumptions and prioritized business cases | Included advisory capacity or a separately scoped assessment |
| Frontier Agent Accelerator | One bounded workflow operating within agreed quality, cost and control limits | Versioned evaluation set, integration tests, acceptance run and operating handover | Included capacity or a separately scoped work package; optional measured improvement fee |
| AI FinOps & Governance Implementation | Defined spend and control coverage with repeatable reporting | Account allocation reconciliation, alerts tested, named owners and agreed reporting cadence | Annual continuing service; additional implementation only where needed |
| Focused Claude advisory | A decision or implementation plan that resolves a named architectural question | Decision record, evaluated options, tradeoffs, practical next steps and stakeholder review | Included advisory capacity or a separately scoped sprint |

An assessment may conclude that the proposed agent should not be built. If the agreed analysis is complete and accepted, that is a valid paid outcome. Do not make advisory fees depend on a favorable recommendation.

## Cost, price and value are different

1. **Adapt delivery cost:** loaded specialist time, direct tools, contractors, project management, evaluation, remediation allowance and allocated delivery overhead.
2. **Customer price:** the agreed base fee plus any earned variable fee. Allocate it to SOW outcomes; do not divide a project fee by a count of unrelated outputs and call that a reusable unit price.
3. **Customer operating cost per successful outcome:** all eligible workflow operating costs divided by unique accepted business completions during the same period. Include failed attempts, retries, review and rework costs in the numerator.
4. **Customer fully loaded cost per outcome:** operating cost plus an explicitly allocated portion of implementation and recurring service fees, divided by accepted outcomes. State the allocation period and volume assumptions. Do not count the same fee twice.
5. **Customer value:** independently agreed benefit, net of incremental operating costs. Reclaimed staff capacity is not cash savings unless spending is actually reduced or capacity is redeployed with evidenced value. Keep forecast value separate from realized value.

Report unit cost as `not measurable` when the accepted count is zero. Missing costs are unknown, never silently zero. Report evidence coverage alongside each metric.

## Quote each account consistently

Use the same method with different account inputs:

- Name the workflow, systems, integrations, environment, users, eligible case types and expected volume.
- Estimate labor by role at loaded delivery cost, plus direct expense, shared delivery overhead and a documented contingency. Include measurement and remediation effort.
- Define `base fee floor = risk-adjusted delivery cost / (1 - target gross margin)`.
- Compare that floor with a conservative, customer-validated benefit case and willingness to pay. There is no universal percentage of value that makes a quote correct. If the economics do not work, reduce scope or decline.
- Adjust the scope and risk allowance for data readiness, integration count, security review, environments, support obligations and dependency uncertainty. Explain scope differences to the customer.
- Allocate separately priced work-package fees to meaningful acceptance milestones. For the annual service, align recurring receipts with the cost of committed capacity. Check both schedules against expected cash outlays.
- Approve any contingent fee separately. Do not rely on it to make an otherwise unprofitable job viable.

### Illustrative economics, not a rate card

| Input | Hypothetical amount |
|---|---:|
| Loaded labor, including delivery overhead | $16,000 |
| Direct project expense | $2,000 |
| Remediation and uncertainty reserve | $2,000 |
| Risk-adjusted delivery cost | $20,000 |
| Proposed minimum gross margin target on the base fee | 45% |
| Calculated base fee floor | $36,363.64 |
| Illustrative quoted base fee | $40,000 |
| Maximum contingent fee | $5,000 |
| Maximum implementation services fee | $45,000 |

At $20,000 delivery cost, gross margin is 50% on the $40,000 base fee, or 55.6% if the full $5,000 contingent fee is earned. At $28,000 actual delivery cost with no contingent fee, it falls to 30%. Gross margin is not net profit; sales, corporate overhead, financing and tax still need coverage. These numbers are arithmetic examples, not evidence of Adapt Cloud's costs or achievable margins.

## Price the continuing service

The scope follows **Discover. Design. Build. Govern. Manage.** Price the advisory, engineering and operating commitments within that method; do not invent a separate commercial delivery lifecycle. Governance effort spans every stage, while Manage funds the agreed reporting, issue response and improvement planning that inform the next Discover cycle.

Price the committed work, not just meetings or access to specialists. The annual scope must reserve capacity for engineering as well as reports and reviews. Start each quarter with an approved 90-day plan; use the monthly review to agree tradeoffs within that capacity. A no-change recommendation needs evidence and a recorded decision. The service fee does not imply an unlimited stream of new features or a guaranteed numerical gain every month.

Report monthly capacity use by advisory, engineering, evaluation and support. Separate planned work, completed work and work blocked by dependencies. The same work cannot consume recurring capacity and be billed under a separately priced work package. Resolve conflicts through the agreed work-item assignment before invoicing.

Annual recurring price must cover contracted advisory and engineering capacity, evaluations, reporting, support coordination, platform entitlement and an appropriate margin. Include platform and subcontractor costs in the delivery estimate. Do not set the recurring fee merely as a percentage of the implementation fee.

For the fictional example, annual advisory and engineering services are $36,000, paid in 12 installments of $3,000. Let `L` be the separately approved first-year PromptForce.AI entitlement price. Base first-year commitment is `$40,000 + $36,000 + L = $76,000 + L`; maximum first-year services and platform commitment is `$81,000 + L` if the $5,000 performance fee is earned. Customer-paid cloud/model usage and applicable taxes are additional. No all-in quote can be issued until `L`, the license rights and usage allowances are confirmed. These are proposed Adapt service numbers, not a DayTwoAI price quote.

A cost floor still applies to recurring services. At a proposed 45% gross margin target, $36,000 supports at most $19,800 of annual risk-adjusted service delivery costs before platform costs if platform is priced separately. Re-estimate against the committed service capacity and escalate if it does not fit. Platform procurement, support escalation, annual evaluation effort and remediation must not disappear from account economics.

Do not repeat implementation fees automatically at renewal. Scope new work separately. Renewal pricing reflects the next term's service capacity, platform entitlement, documented usage and agreed improvement plan. Keep unearned outcome bonuses out of the base renewal business case.

## Is the outcome measurable enough to price?

Use all six gates for a variable fee:

| Gate | Required agreement |
|---|---|
| Definition | One observable success event, eligible population, unique business ID and reopen rule |
| Baseline | Signed measurement period, population, task mix and data sources |
| Control | Adapt owns the material changes; customer and third-party dependencies are named |
| Evidence | Accessible source records, cost coverage, minimum volume and a reproducible calculation |
| Quality | Quality, security and human-review guardrails must pass alongside speed or cost |
| Adjudication | Named approver, review deadline, dispute process, cure limit and payment rule |

If any gate is absent, use a fixed discovery or delivery fee and track the hoped-for business benefit separately.

| Outcome | Tracking difficulty | Appropriate promise |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluation and handover completed to a signed rubric | Low | Fixed accepted milestone |
| Spend mapped to agreed owners and reconciled to invoices | Low to medium | Fixed coverage target, with missing sources visible |
| Accepted document processing with review/reopen rules | Medium | Bounded success and unit-cost targets |
| Handling time reduced without quality regression | Medium | Matched population and actual review time |
| Realized cloud savings | Medium to high | Normalize volume, rates, scope and unrelated changes |
| New revenue or profit growth | High | Track business value; avoid a default payment dependency |
| Zero breaches or guaranteed regulatory compliance | Not a bounded, demonstrable delivery result | Test specified controls; do not make blanket guarantees |

## Keep measurement economical

Begin with one primary workflow KPI, two or three guardrails and an account-level outcome register. A weekly review should check evidence, exceptions, approvals and the invoice position. Reserve delivery capacity for instrumentation and review; do not assume tracking is free. If measurement costs more than the disputed variable fee, simplify the commercial model.

Record every business case once, link all attempts to it, and count success only after its acceptance and reopen window. Keep quality acceptance separate from whether a human helped. A human-assisted result can count if the SOW allows it, but its labor cost and review time must be included.

For savings, lock comparable volume, case mix and the agreed unit-price basis. Show actual billed cost separately from a price-normalized engineering comparison. Model vendor price cuts, discounts, discontinued workloads and customer process changes must not be credited to Adapt unless the parties expressly agree. Use a comparison cohort or staged rollout where feasible; a before/after comparison alone does not establish causation.

For model changes, preserve the old baseline, record the change, rerun evaluations and agree whether rebaselining is needed. Never rewrite historical results to make the target appear achieved.

## Rollout for Adapt Cloud

Use this structure on the next small set of qualified accounts. Track actual delivery cost, gross margin, payment time, remediation hours and the effort spent proving results. Refine scope bands from those observations. Continue to sell specialist Claude advisory and engineering on judgment, integration and verified delivery; certification or partner designations should appear only when separately verified.
