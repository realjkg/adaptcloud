The compliance engine your DIB practice delivers with

Issue 1.0 Date 2026-09-06 Audience Co-delivery partner evaluation Scope NIST SP 800-171 R2 · CMMC L2

Phase II is suspended. The obligation is not. DFARS 252.204-7012, Phase I self-assessment, and SPRS score submission against NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 all remain in force. What went away was the certification deadline — not the requirement that a contractor's score be current, evidenced, and defensible.

That shift is bad news for anyone selling assessment urgency and good news for anyone selling continuous evidence. Your defense clients still need their posture proven on a rolling basis, and your practice can bill that work today. The constraint is that each engagement is hand-built by senior people, so margin is capped by bench hours and the tenth engagement costs about what the first one did.

AdaptCloud is the engine that breaks that curve. It is the IP layer underneath a readiness or managed-compliance offer: your consultants run it, your name is on the delivery, and engagement N+1 costs materially less to produce than engagement N.

Differentiator

IP

A versioned control library, host scanner rules, remediation playbooks, and governed cloud landing-zone modules. Owned, not assembled per client.

Solution

Scan, remediate, re-verify — a closed loop that produces timestamped evidence, not a checklist someone filled in from memory.

Delivery

Fixed-fee engagement scaffolds your bench executes. We supply the engine and the enablement; the client relationship stays yours.

Those are not scannable by anyone, and a vendor implying otherwise is telling your assessor something that will not survive contact. The honest split is the sales asset: the technically verifiable controls are automated and re-verified continuously; the rest are delivered by your people, faster, because the technical half is already evidenced.

Coverage is the roadmap. Every engagement is expected to return a control check, a remediation rule, or a reference module to the library, which is what compounds across your client base rather than being spent on one of them.

Why an assessor trusts the output

Every finding carries its provenance

AI drafts the narrative. That is a real efficiency and a real risk, because a generated SSP paragraph and an observed system fact look identical on the page. They are separated in the data model, so they can never be conflated in a bundle.

Machine-observed

The scanner read the live configuration. Carries a host, a timestamp, and the check that produced it.

Model-asserted

Drafted from context by the AI layer. Useful, reviewable, and never presented as an observation.

Human-attested

A named person signed it. Required before any model-asserted item enters a submitted package.

Data boundary

The engine does not hold the client's CUI

The scanner runs on the customer's host and returns control status, findings and scores. Configuration artifacts, system files and CUI stay on the customer's side of the line. This is a deliberate architectural constraint, not a current limitation: it is what keeps both parties out of the obligations that attach to a cloud service storing or processing CUI under DFARS 252.204-7012, and it is designed in before the first contract rather than remediated after it.

Where a client wants a fully hosted posture, that is a scoped conversation with its own compliance path, priced separately, and not what this brief proposes.

What this brief does not claim

  • Not a FedRAMP-authorized service. No authorization is held or asserted, and the architecture is designed so none is required for the proposed scope.
  • Not a C3PAO and not an assessment. The engine produces evidence. It does not certify, and it does not substitute for an authorized assessor.
  • 93 practices are not automated. Six of fourteen domains have automated checks today. The rest are human-delivered, and the coverage figure is published rather than implied.
  • No CUI is processed by the engine. Findings and scores cross the boundary; artifacts do not.
  • No past-performance claim is made in this document. Reference engagements will be named under a mutual NDA, or not at all.

Proposed first step

One shared client, sixty days, defined exit criteria

  1. Select one mutual DIB client with an active 800-171 obligation and a host estate under 25 machines.
  2. Joint scan and baseline. Your consultants run the engine; we sit behind them. Output is a scored, provenance-tagged evidence bundle.
  3. Remediate and re-verify the technically automated controls, then hand the human-delivered domains to your practice on the accelerated timeline the technical evidence buys.
  4. Measure the library ratio — the share of the delivery that came from existing components versus written fresh. That single number decides whether this scales for you, and it is the criterion we both agree to judge the pilot on.
