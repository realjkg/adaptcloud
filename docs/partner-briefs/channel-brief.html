Executive summary

  1. A recurring, mandatory obligation remains in place following the suspension of CMMC Phase II on 13 July 2026.1 Phase I self-assessment, DFARS 252.204-7012, NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 and submission of a score to the Supplier Performance Risk System were unaffected.2
  2. Category demand has been displaced rather than removed. Propositions constructed around the November 2026 assessment date have lost their close, while the underlying compliance requirement continues to attach to contract award and recurs annually. A catalogue position framed on continuous evidence rather than certification faces reduced competition in the near term.
  3. The proposed entry price is set below procurement-review thresholds at typical small suppliers. The $990 annual tier represents under one per cent of an indicative first compliance cycle (Exhibit 1), which supports transaction on a single purchase order rather than a managed sales cycle.
  4. No federal contract vehicle is presently held. This is stated at section 5 and again at section 7. Vehicle strategy is a principal reason for the discussion and is proposed as joint work rather than as a precondition to listing.
  5. The proposed first step is a 90-day listing test on the two self-service stock-keeping units, with no services dependency, assessed against registration and quotation volume rather than revenue.
2

Demand basis

The Department suspended Phase II of the CMMC programme on 13 July 2026, deferring the third-party assessment requirement scheduled for 10 November 2026 and constituting a reform task force.1 Self-assessment obligations under Phase I, the DFARS safeguarding clause, and the underlying NIST standard were not suspended.2

The commercial consequence for a catalogue is that the addressable requirement persists and renews, while the urgency that had been driving competitor sales motions has been withdrawn. Suppliers must continue to maintain and submit a current score, and that score degrades as the estate drifts from its assessed configuration, which establishes an annual re-engagement event independent of the assessment regime.

3

Offering description

The offering comprises a host assessment binary, a versioned control library, and infrastructure-as-code modules for governed cloud deployment. The binary assesses a host against the control library, applies remediation on an explicit flag, re-verifies the result and emits machine-readable evidence.

Automated coverage is seventeen practices across six of the fourteen control domains at the date of issue, against 110 practices at Level 2. The remaining domains are predominantly policy, training and process controls, a substantial proportion of which are not susceptible to automated verification by any vendor. This figure is published in the enablement materials and disclosed at qualification rather than characterised in general terms; the reputational exposure from an overstated scope is realised during a client's assessment, at which point the account and the reference are lost together.

The cloud modules provide a governed application platform with policy-as-code enforcement and discrete workload identities for AI agents, aligned to published cloud adoption framework guidance. This addresses AI agent governance, an emerging control requirement in the buyer base for which established patterns are limited, and is assessed as the least substitutable element of the offering.

Each item in an evidence bundle carries a provenance classification of machine-observed, model-asserted or human-attested. Generative AI is applied to narrative drafting and is never presented as observation. The separation is enforced in the data model and is the basis on which an AI-assisted submission remains defensible under assessment.

4

Catalogue structure

Metering is on assessment scope, expressed as hosts, and not on user seats. Scope is the unit the compliance buyer already reasons in, and seat metering is assessed as adverse to penetration of the small-supplier segment that constitutes the volume opportunity.

The renewal profile follows the obligation: annual, recurring, with a natural re-engagement event at each cycle. The catalogue is accordingly structured as renewing subscription rather than perpetual licence.

Channel enablement

  • E1Demonstration environment. Hosted reference tool and a scripted assessment-to-evidence walkthrough executable by a sales engineer in approximately fifteen minutes without access to a client host.
  • E2Qualification script. Four questions establishing whether a prospect holds an active 800-171 obligation, a current SPRS score, and hosts within scope.

Recommended next step

A 90-day listing test on the two self-service references, structured in four workstreams.

  • W1List AC-SCAN-10 and AC-HARDEN-25 as a low-friction test of catalogue pull carrying no services dependency.
  • W2Operate the reference tool as a demand capture asset against the existing defence supplier base, measured on registrations rather than revenue through the first 60 days.
  • W3Select the contract vehicle path jointly and commence the qualification it requires, in parallel with listing.
  • W4Resolve the enclave band to a single list position on the basis of observed deployment effort.

Review point. At 90 days, against registration volume, quotation requests and conversion, the parties determine whether the enclave and services references join the catalogue.
