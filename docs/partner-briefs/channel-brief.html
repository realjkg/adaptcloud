AdaptCloud · Channel Brief

A compliance SKU for a market that still has the obligation

Issue 1.0 Date 2026-09-06 Audience Public sector distribution evaluation Category Cybersecurity compliance software

The deadline moved. The requirement did not. With Phase II suspended, a large share of the defense industrial base has quietly stopped buying certification-driven products — while Phase I self-assessment, DFARS 252.204-7012 and SPRS score submission against NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 all remain mandatory.

That gap is the channel opportunity. Every vendor whose pitch was a November date has lost its close, and the buyers still carry an unmet, recurring obligation with no urgency attached. A product priced for continuous evidence rather than a one-time certification sprint has an open field and a renewing revenue line.

The price point is deliberately below the threshold that triggers a procurement review at a small or mid-size subcontractor. It closes on a card or a single purchase order, which is the transaction profile that actually moves volume through a catalog rather than through a sales cycle.

Metering is by hosts in scope, never by user seat. Compliance buyers already reason in scope, and seat pricing penalizes exactly the small subcontractors this catalog is meant to reach. Cloud consumption is never bundled — where a customer's own Azure or Google Cloud spend is involved it is quoted separately and billed by them, so no SKU carries a pass-through that inflates the catalog price against comparable products.

Renewal profile: the underlying obligation is annual and recurring, and a customer's SPRS score decays as their estate drifts. This is a renewing subscription with a natural re-engagement event every twelve months, not a perpetual license with an attach-rate problem.

Product substance

What a buyer actually receives

A Go binary that scans a host against a versioned control library, applies remediation, re-verifies the result, and emits machine-readable evidence — 17 practices automated across 6 of the 14 domains today, with the remaining domains delivered by people. That figure is on the coverage sheet and in the sales conversation, because a compliance product caught overstating its scope loses the account and the reference at the same time.

Alongside it: Terraform for a governed cloud landing zone with policy-as-code and per-agent workload identities, which addresses AI agent governance — a control problem defense buyers are acquiring right now and have no established pattern for.

Every item in an evidence bundle is tagged as machine-observed, model-asserted or human-attested. AI drafts narrative; it never poses as an observation. That separation is what makes an AI-assisted package survive an assessor's review.

Stated limits

  • No contract vehicle is held today. Stated above and repeated here so it cannot be missed in a catalog review.
  • Not a C3PAO. The product produces evidence; it does not certify and does not replace an authorized assessment.
  • 93 of 110 practices are not automated. Many never can be by any vendor, being policy and process rather than configuration.
  • No FedRAMP authorization is held or claimed. The catalog SKUs are architected not to require one.
  • No customer references are named in this document. They will be provided under NDA when they exist, and not before.

Proposed first step

Catalog listing on two SKUs, ninety days

  1. List the two self-serve SKUs — the ten-host and twenty-five-host tiers — as a low-friction test of catalog pull with no services dependency.
  2. Run the free lookup tool as a demand capture asset against the distributor's existing defense contractor base, measuring registrations rather than revenue in the first sixty days.
  3. Select the vehicle path jointly and begin whichever qualification it requires, in parallel with the listing rather than as a precondition.
  4. Review at ninety days against registrations, quote requests and conversion — and decide together whether the enclave and services SKUs join the catalog.
