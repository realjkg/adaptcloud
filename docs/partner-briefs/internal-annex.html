Purpose and scope

This annex records two items removed from the partner briefs at version 1.0, the position on each, and the point at which each becomes disclosable. It exists so that withheld material remains tracked and owned rather than merely absent, and so that the difference between the internal and external positions is documented rather than reconstructed later.

Neither item was removed because the position is unfavorable. Both were removed because they are unresolved internal matters that a counterparty cannot act on, and their presence in a first-issue partner document invited scrutiny of a decision not yet taken. Section 4 records when each must be surfaced.

2

Item 1 — Engine license position

The audit engine repository is published under the GNU General Public License version 3. That license imposes source-disclosure obligations on distribution of the software but does not extend to provision of the software as a network service. A third party may therefore operate a competing hosted service derived from the engine, without disclosure and without payment, and the hosted tiers at Exhibit 4 of the co-delivery brief carry no licensing barrier to that outcome.

2.1 Position on remedy

The two conventional remedies are the GNU Affero General Public License, which extends the disclosure obligation to network use, and a business source license with a delayed open-source conversion date. Either forecloses the substitution described above. The choice is a commercial one: the Affero license retains open-source status and community distribution, whereas a business source license permits a use restriction for a defined period but is not an open-source license and is treated as proprietary by many procurement functions.

2.2 Why this is time-sensitive

Relicensing requires the agreement of every copyright holder in the codebase. Enumeration of the repository history as at the date of issue shows seven commits and two contributing identities, one of which is an automated coding agent whose output ownership is governed by the platform's terms of service and should be confirmed with counsel rather than assumed. The consent perimeter is therefore presently narrow and administratively trivial to clear.

That position degrades permanently on the first external contribution. Each subsequent contributor acquires copyright in their contribution and becomes a necessary party to any relicensing, and a contributor who is unreachable or unwilling cannot be worked around except by removing and rewriting their contribution. The cost of this decision is close to zero today and rises without bound.

Recommendation
Relicense the engine to AGPL-3.0, or adopt a business source license with a defined conversion term, before any external contribution is accepted. Introduce a contributor license agreement at the same time.
Owner
AdaptCloud principal, with counsel input on agent-generated contribution ownership
Decide by
Before the repository accepts its first external pull request, and before execution of any partner agreement referencing hosted delivery
If deferred
The hosted tiers remain substitutable by any competent third party, which materially weakens the product-led position the commercial construct rests on

Item 2 — Enclave price band

The enclave tier is quoted externally as a range, described in both briefs as varying with the number of landing zones and workload environments in scope. That description is accurate. What is withheld is that the band has not been validated against observed deployment effort, because no enclave deployment has yet been delivered at commercial scope. The range is a construction from component cost and estimated effort rather than a measurement.

3.1 Basis of the current figure

Reference infrastructure cost is documented in the repository at three postures, from approximately $80 to $150 per month at demonstration settings, through module defaults, to several thousand per month at production settings, driven principally by API gateway tier, search unit count and private endpoint count. That cost is the client's and is excluded from the AdaptCloud price under assumption A3. The band therefore expresses deployment and governance effort only, and that effort is the unmeasured quantity.

3.2 What would resolve it

  • M1Record practitioner hours to first governed landing zone at production posture, separated into platform establishment, policy assignment and workload onboarding.
  • M2Record the incremental hours for each additional workload environment, which is the variable the band is actually indexed to.
  • M3Restate as a fixed platform establishment fee plus a per-environment increment, which removes the range without requiring a single blended figure.
Recommendation
Retain the range externally until M1 and M2 are recorded on a first delivery. Restate per M3 rather than narrowing the range by estimate.
